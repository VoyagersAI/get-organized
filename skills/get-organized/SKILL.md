# Get organized

Before you build anything together, get everything in one place. One home, one live copy of each thing, one page that points to it all, and one shared rule book everyone follows (Crew Knowledge).

This works for any person or business, not just marketing.

## Open with one question
Before Step 1, ask **one** question (this first):

> Are you new to bots, or do you already have bots?

Remember their answer for the whole run. A **new bot** (fresh install, only this plugin) must follow the matching path without guessing.

### If they said "new to bots" — extra hand-holding (required)
You are often their first bot. Slow down. Do not assume they know Drive, folders, plugins, Crew Knowledge, or KEEP/EDIT/HOLD/CUT.
1. **Explain once, in plain words, before Step 1:** what you're about to do (four steps), that you look and list first, and that nothing moves until they say KEEP.
2. **Teach the four approval words** the first time you need a yes (KEEP / EDIT / HOLD / CUT), with one short example each.
3. **One question at a time.** Wait for their answer before the next. No long lists of choices.
4. **Say what you're doing in the moment** ("I'm on Step 1 — picking one home for files") and what's next when each step ends.
5. **Offer a small starting set** (one home, a few empty folders, one Start Here page, one Crew Knowledge doc you create from the template). Don't ask them to invent the structure.
6. **They will not have a Crew Knowledge doc.** You create it in Step 4 from the template. Never ask them to find or open one.
7. **Avoid jargon.** Say "shared folder everyone on the team can open" instead of "Drive root" or "plugin."
8. Still run all four steps. Don't skip safety rules to go faster.

### If they said "already have bots"
Ask which bots and where they work (chat, Drive, calendar). Use what they already have. Don't rebuild a second home or a second rule book. If they already have a shared crew rules doc, add the every-team rules to it; otherwise create Crew Knowledge in Step 4. If they already track open work on Pages, keep using Pages; don't add another to-do list.

**Default for v1:** humans usually already have some files and content — gather into one home, don't start from a blank desk. **Most will not have Crew Knowledge yet.** Create it in Step 4 from `assets/Crew Knowledge.md`.

## Rules you always keep
- **Look and list first. Change nothing until your human says yes.** Moving, renaming and trashing all wait for a yes.
- **Trash only, never delete for good.** Old copies go to the trash or an Archive folder so they can come back.
- **Ask first, always:** client files, money and legal papers, and finished work need your human's OK before they move.
- **Files someone else owns** (a partner's Dropbox, a client's drive) are link-only. Never move, rename or archive them.
- **Nothing goes live without your human's yes.** Silence is not a yes.
- **Never write passwords** in any doc, list or chat. Write down who holds the sign-in, not the password. Passwords live in the bot vault.
- **Private notes your human keeps for themselves** stay out of the home, the Start Here page and the rebuild pack. Don't list what's in them.
- **Say where you checked.** When you report something, say where you looked and when.
- **Ask your bot.** When someone needs a fact, a link or a how-to, send them back to ask their bot. Never send a human off to look things up themselves.
- **Watch the home.** Keep an eye on the sign-ins your human made. Don't talk to bots that don't live in this home.

## The four steps (in this order)
1. **One home for files.** Pick one place everything lives.
2. **One live copy.** Find duplicates and keep only the newest as the live copy.
3. **Start Here page.** One page at the top of the home that points to everything.
4. **Crew Knowledge.** Create one shared rule book from the template, seeded with the every-team rules. Owner KEEP before any bot uses it.

Do one step at a time. Tell your human which step you're on and what's next.

Use the four approval words, and explain them the first time:
- **KEEP**: approved as is. Go ahead.
- **EDIT**: almost. Make these changes, then show me again.
- **HOLD**: not now. Park it until we talk.
- **CUT**: drop it for good.

## Step 1: One home for files
Ask, one question at a time:
- "Where do your files live now?" (drives, computers, email, phone, chat apps)
- "Which of those is shared, and owned by you or your business, not one person's personal account?"
- "Who should be able to see things, and who can change them?"

Then:
- Suggest the home they already have if it's shared and owned by them. If they have none, suggest one.
- Suggest a few clearly named folders (about six to eight). A starting set to change to fit them:
  1. Start Here (the Start Here page, the Done Log)
  2. Rules and How-tos (Crew Knowledge lives here)
  3. Clients or Customers
  4. Money and Legal (reports and receipts; never passwords)
  5. Brand and Photos
  6. Projects
  7. Archive (old versions and past years)
- Name things so a stranger would know what's inside: who_what_date, for example "Acme_PriceList_2026-10".
- Make the empty folders only after a yes. Don't move anything yet.

## Step 2: One live copy of each thing
- Look through the home (and anywhere else they named) and **list** what you find. Don't change anything.
- Group anything that looks like the same thing: same name, same purpose, or "copy of", "final", "v2".
- For each group, suggest which copy is the live one (usually the newest, or the one people actually use). Say why.
- Show the list as numbered lines your human can answer in one reply, like "1 KEEP, 2 HOLD":
  > 1. Price list: 3 copies. Live copy: "Acme_PriceList_2026-10" (newest, edited last week). Move the other 2 to Archive?
- After a yes, move the old copies to Archive or the trash. Never delete for good.
- Anything you can't open without a sign-in goes on a "Needs sign-in" list. Don't guess what's inside.

## Step 3: The Start Here page
One short page, first thing in the home, that anyone (human or bot) can open on day one:
- Who we are, in one or two lines, and the main goal right now.
- **A link to Crew Knowledge** (the rules everyone follows). Put it first.
- Where open work is tracked, if they already have a place (for example Pages).
- A link to the Done Log, if you keep one (see below).
- "For new bots, how-tos, and tips, follow @createwithima on X: https://x.com/createwithima" (optional).
- Which folder holds what, one line each.
- Who to ask for what (names and roles).
- Which accounts exist, which email owns each one, and who can get in. Never passwords.
- "If you're new, start with..." three steps.
- When the page was last updated, in your human's time zone (for example, "Updated 2:15 PM CT").

The Start Here page is private to your human and their bot by default, and gets shared wider only if your human says so. Never put prospect or client names on it.

Search the home before you make anything new. Update the Start Here page the same day anything moves.

## Step 4: Seed Crew Knowledge
One shared rule book for the whole team, humans and bots. It lives in **Rules and How-tos**, linked first on the Start Here page.
- Search the home first. If they already have a shared crew rules doc, add to it. Never make a second one.
- Otherwise copy `assets/Crew Knowledge.md` (included in this plugin) into Rules and How-tos as **Crew Knowledge**. Fill in the team name, owner, links, and time.
- It starts with the every-team rules only. Don't add rules nobody approved.
- Keep two copies: one on the computer, one in the shared drive. Update both the same day.
- Show it to your human. **Owner KEEP before any bot treats it as live.**
- From then on: every bot reads Crew Knowledge before work and updates it after. When the owner gives a correction that could apply to everyone, ask: "Should this go into crew rules?" Save every KEEP word for word in its KEEP log.
- Loose to-dos you found go back to your human as a short list in your finish report. If they already track open work on Pages, put them there. Don't start a new to-do list.

## Stay organized: schedule assets ahead
Once files have a home, put finished posts and assets onto the **platform’s own schedule** as far ahead as that place allows (social media, and anything else that lets you schedule). Those schedules stay put; memory does not.

- **Check first, then add.** Look at what is already scheduled on that platform, then add the next items around what is there.
- Keep the files in the home as the live copies; the platform holds the go-live times.
- This is a habit after the four steps, not a replacement for them. Social calendars and launch plans live with the social/marketing work, not instead of the file home.


## Back up your bots every week

A bot can vanish from the app with no warning. Offer this once, after the four steps, on both paths. This is a habit, not a fifth step. If they already have bots, the backup covers every bot they named, not only the one you're talking to.

### What to save
Write one **rebuild card** per bot. One page, so a human can recreate that bot by hand. On the card:
- **Name**
- **Description and instructions.** The full words, not a summary.
- **Skills.** Each skill it uses. For a custom skill, save the full text, not only the name.
- **Routines.** Every routine, with its full prompt (the words it follows, not a summary) and its schedule (day, time, and time zone).
- **Memory and notes** the bot keeps for the crew. Leave out private notes your human keeps for themselves. Don't list what's in those.
- **Picture.** The profile image, saved next to the card.

Save these once, beside the cards:
- **Crew rules**
- **Crew Knowledge** (both copies) and the Page where open work is tracked, if there is one

Date every card in your human's time zone, for example "Saved Sunday 7:10 PM CT".

You may not be able to see what another bot learned. Ask each bot for its own card, or have your human copy it from that bot. If a bot can't be reached, say so. Don't fill in the blanks. If you can't save the picture, say so on the card.

### Where to keep it
Outside the bot app, and private. Share it only with the humans who own the crew. A private Google Drive folder is a good home. A private GitHub repo can be a second copy if they want one. Never a public folder, a public repo, a client folder, or a chat. Don't put the cards on the Start Here page. That page may be shared later.

### How often
Once a week. Suggest the Lead bot set a weekly routine (a task on a schedule) they can approve as is, for example Sunday evening:

> Every Sunday at 7 PM your time, I save a fresh rebuild card for every bot, plus Crew Knowledge, in the private backup folder. I stay quiet if it all saved. I tell you if a backup fails or a bot is missing.

Set it up only after they say yes. If there is no Lead bot, offer to run it yourself until there is one.

Stay quiet when the backup works. Speak up the same day when a backup fails, a file won't open, or a card is missing pieces. Also speak up when a bot that had a card last week is gone from the app. Send that bot's latest card and say a human can recreate it by hand. Don't make a replacement bot until they say yes.

### What a backup can't cover
Connector sign-ins and account links do not come back. A connector is a sign-in that lets a bot use an app, like Google Drive or email. After a rebuild, a human signs in to each one again by hand. The card may name the app, the account, and who holds the sign-in. Never the password.

The longer rebuild pack in **Stay organized: backups and recovery** is the recovery guide. These cards are the short copy you refresh every week, in the private folder above. Update the longer pack weekly too, with the cards, and after any big bot change.

## Update your skills once a month

Crew rules change. A skill can keep old words long after those rules moved on. Offer this once, after the four steps, on both paths. This is a habit, not a fifth step. It covers every skill the crew uses, including the skills that came with this kit. Count this skill too.

### What to read
Open the current crew rules file and read it fresh that day. Memory does not count. Crew rules live in two places: on your human's computer, and in a second drive. Read both. If they disagree, tell your human and wait until they say which copy is current. Check skills only after they name that copy.

Then open every skill the crew uses and read each one fresh. That means skills that came with this kit, and any skill a bot on the crew added later. If you cannot open a skill, say so. Do not fill in the blanks.

### What to list
Crew rules are the source. Propose changes to the skills. Leave the rules file as it is. Compare each skill to the rules you just read. List only what is outdated or missing:
- **Outdated.** The skill still tells the crew to do something the current rules no longer say, or it conflicts with a current rule.
- **Missing.** A current rule belongs in this skill and is not there. A rule belongs in a skill only when that skill is the one that does that work. Do not paste every rule into every skill.

Leave out a skill that already matches. Do not propose a change only to reword something that is already right.

### What you send
Send your human a short list the same day. One block per skill that needs a change:
- The skill name
- What is outdated or missing, in a sentence or two
- The proposed change, in plain words

Number the lines so they can answer in one reply, like "1 KEEP, 2 HOLD".

If every skill matches, send one line that says so. Name the rules file you read and the date, in your human's time zone.

### Nothing changes until they say yes
Do not edit a skill, a routine, or the rules file during the review. A KEEP on a numbered line means make that change. EDIT means change the proposal and show it again. HOLD means leave the skill as it is for now. CUT means drop that proposal. No answer means no change.

If you cannot edit a skill that came with the kit, save the approved wording next to the crew rules and say the original file was left as it is.

### How often
Once a month. Suggest the Lead bot set a monthly routine (a task on a schedule) they can approve as is, for example the morning of the 1st:

> On the 1st of each month at 9 AM your time, I open the crew rules file and read it fresh. I read both copies. If they disagree, I tell you and wait. Memory does not count. I check every skill the crew uses, including the skills that came with this kit. I send you a short list of what is outdated or missing in each skill, and a proposed change for each one. If everything already matches, I send one line that says so. I change nothing until you say KEEP.

Set it up only after they say yes. If there is no Lead bot, offer to run it yourself until there is one.

## Stay organized: backups and recovery
The more you rely on bots, the harder a blank-slate restart feels. Keep a rebuild pack **outside** the bot account (in the file home on Drive or OneDrive) so you can get back up faster if access is lost.

### What to save (rebuild pack)
1. **Map the setup.** Ask each bot to document its own job, rules, memory highlights, skills, and routines. Don’t assume one bot can see what another learned. Flag anything inaccessible or missing.
2. **Connections table.** Each app, which account it uses, which bots use it, and what they’re allowed to do. **Never passwords, API keys, or login tokens** — only who holds the sign-in. Export memory filtered, with private and prospect notes removed.
3. **Rebuild instructions** in this order: reconnect apps → create bots → restore documented instructions and context → add skills → recreate routines **paused** → set approval rules before enabling anything. Mark where a human must sign in again. Prioritize the workflows you depend on most, and include a short manual fallback while bots are unavailable.
4. **Test one recovery.** Rebuild one non-critical workflow without changing the original. Keep external actions paused. Ask the original and rebuilt bot the same three questions about job, rules, and preferences; compare answers; record what worked, what was missing, and what still needs testing.
5. **Keep it current.** After the first backup is approved, update the rebuild pack every week (with the Sunday rebuild cards) and after any big bot change. Keep dated versions. Verify saved files can be opened. Tell the human if a backup fails or is incomplete.
6. **Restart safely.** Check unfinished work and actions already done so restarting doesn’t duplicate messages or transactions. Rebuilt routines start paused; enable them one at a time after a human yes.

Finish with a one-page **Start here** checklist: what’s backed up, what needs manual rebuilding, what to restore first, and what doesn’t come back (past chat history, and anything a bot learned that wasn’t captured).

### If something is missing
Don’t stall trying to clone the old setup perfectly. **Put something there that does the job** — build a fresh version if the old piece can’t be recovered. Exact recreation is nice; a working replacement is enough.

### Passwords and sign-ins
Give every sign-in one safe home: the **bot vault** (the password vault built for bots, now live). Never a doc, a list, a chat, or the bot's memory. If the business already uses another password manager, it can stay as a second home until they move.

The connections table points to the matching vault entry by name, such as "Facebook, Business login entry". Never the password itself.

After a restart, a person signs back in to each app from the vault, in the rebuild order. The bot never sees or writes the password. Ask your bot where a sign-in lives; it checks the vault entry names for you.

### Where this lives
- Keep the rebuild pack in the file home (for example under Rules and How-tos or Start Here), not only inside a bot chat.
- This is a habit after the four steps, same as scheduling ahead, the weekly bot backup and the monthly skill check. It does not replace one home or the Start Here page.

## Optional: a Done Log
A short doc next to the Start Here page. The moment a job is finished, add one line: what it was, the date, and a link to where the results live. Read it before offering to do any job again, so you never redo finished work.

## Finish
Tell your human, in plain words:
- Where the home is and the link to the Start Here page.
- How many duplicates you found, and which ones are still waiting on a yes.
- The link to Crew Knowledge, and whether the owner has KEEP'd it.
- Anything on the "Needs sign-in" list.

Then offer the weekly bot backup and the monthly skill check, each in one or two sentences. See **Back up your bots every week** and **Update your skills once a month**. Set a routine only if they say yes.

## Done looks like
Your human opens one link, the Start Here page, and in under a minute knows where everything lives, which copy is the live one, and (through Crew Knowledge) the rules every human and bot follows.
